# Who this game is for: David

The goal (David, Nov 19): "At all costs make the game for David to enjoy. 30 min prototype that makes David say
'Yes, I want more in a real game I play for hours over the course of months with Claude.'" Use everything he has told
us. If we can't write, draw or design something well enough ourselves, find free-to-use work online (CC0 / public
domain, credited). This file is the checklist every part of the game is judged against.

## What he loves (his words, paraphrased)
- **SMT**: SMT I (the Law/Chaos/Neutral companions with separate goals; the personality results; an ending built
  around you and your choices, good or bad), SMT V (slime-tier bad early demons; Pixie as the early healer).
- **Real decisions that change the plot**, Fire Emblem: Three Houses style: pick a side, support a leader at a key
  moment and they trust you more, temper them by being there; betray; try peace talks that fail; reach out to other
  factions who respond based on what you did — including things you didn't know you chose.
- **Catherine's ending**: at the very end the game tells you its theme and what your choices say about you.
- **Fixed events with open outcomes**: like the bombs in SMT I — something big happens no matter what, but who is
  where, who lives, who betrays you, how you prepared changes everything. Never stuck with an unwinnable bad ending.
- **Hidden meters** tracking things he doesn't think about: stats he picks, how much he talks to demons, which
  demons he uses, which NPCs he talks to first, how often he runs. NPCs react to them.
- **NPCs who lie**; missing a lie bites you (or accidentally saves you).
- **Claude must not just do whatever he says**: the world has its own truth (the bible) and pushes back.
- **Free-form demon talk**, no menus: quotes are speech, everything else is action; flatter, flirt, beg, trick, mug,
  walk away. Demons talk wildly differently: one needs the bathroom and leaves if you say yes; one talks like a child;
  one is a garbled mess; one asks if you're a top or a bottom; some talk first; some comment on your past deeds.
- **Gay protagonist, homoerotic vibe**: adult male demons are gay if it comes up; telling a snake-bodied giant he
  looks strong and virile and winking. Never explicit. ("I never see that in SMT since they don't cater to gay men,
  but this game should.")
- **Press-turn strategy**: chaining weaknesses and passes into sweeps before the enemy acts; demons with one
  all-target element move; protecting your own weaknesses.
- **Armor that changes talking** (more flirt/recruit odds with certain demons, less defense — a skimpy/crotchless
  joke piece), **varied dungeon demons** (never the same 4), varied fun monsters.

## What he dislikes
- Repetitive demons (the kappa that kept pushing its riddle), meeting the same few demons over and over.
- Law vs chaos with a "true" neutral ending; good vs evil; obvious themes; contrived themes ("mostly contrived").
- Being forced into an unpreventable bad ending halfway through.
- Plots where whatever he says simply happens.

## The theme (his note)
**Taken to the extreme, you can't have room for everything.** Several positions that exclude each other (you can't
be both; neutral is its own position with its own cost: indecision, helping no one; and there are third and fourth
options that reject the whole frame), each with a real cost when pushed all the way. Not law/chaos. Not obvious.

## So the prototype must
1. Open fast and funny/strange, with a demon talking within the first 2-3 minutes.
2. Make him choose between people he likes, early, and show consequences later in the same 30 minutes.
3. Track his behavior silently and have NPCs and demons call it out ("you ran from three fights").
4. Contain at least one lie that matters and can be caught.
5. Let him flirt, bribe, trick and mug, with demons reacting in character, including a top/bottom moment and a
   bathroom ruse.
6. Have demons that feel distinct in talk AND in battle (signature moves, odd weaknesses, one bad early demon,
   one Pixie-like healer).
7. End with his own words deciding the outcome, then an SMT I/Catherine-style reflection on the theme and on him.
8. Look good: consistent CC0 pixel art for maps and sprites, museum art for demon portraits, battle effects.
