# Playtest 1: The Flood Ward (AI playtester, headless, scripted mock brain)

Played Nov 19, 19:22–20:20 UTC (~58 min real time, with tool overhead) from New Game to the ending "The River Comes Home".
I played the way docs/DAVID.md describes him: talked to everyone, flirted (Dario, Furfur, Troll, Oni, Ares, Gonggong),
bought the Strappy Harness, caught Dario's lie, paid off and tricked demons, fused a Valkyrie, and decided the finale in my
own words. The ending screen recorded: spoke with 21, recruited 8, killed 30, ran from 13, let 7 walk away, caught 2 lies,
Lv 11.

Caveat: demon and NPC free-talk replies came from the keyword mock. I don't judge reply quality or variety below, only
the rules around them.

## Verdict (5 lines)
1. **Yes, but not yet.** The scripted writing, Dario, Furfur's "top or bottom?", the kettle, the hidden-meter callouts and the Catherine-style coda are exactly what David asked for.
2. The middle hour drags. The same eight demons keep coming back (Leprechaun ×5, Ares ×5, Oni ×4, Golem ×4, Charon ×4, Bake-danuki ×4), a random fight starts every 6–12 steps, and the game forgets every weakness you found once the fight ends.
3. The Undergate Approach is a wall. Lv 11–12 random enemies, no spring at the entrance, and failed escapes that hand the enemy a free turn killed me twice in normal play. The Strappy Harness (Physical weak) makes it much worse, and you can only take it off at the shop in town, three floors up.
4. The best consequence scenes (Pell's reunion with Ines, warning Sump Row, confronting Hester) only trigger if you walk back up to the Ward, and nothing tells you to. I went straight down, missed all of them, and got "not everyone got out" even though I typed "Sump Row gets a warning first" into the finale.
5. Fix the encounter rate, weakness memory, the Undergate spike and the signposting of the return trip, and David would want more. As it stands he probably quits in the Undergate, or finishes feeling the ending ignored his specific choices.

## What was fun
- **The opening (first 60 seconds).** "Morning, love. Don't touch the kettle." / Kettle: "Good morning! Do touch the kettle. Gently. Round the spout." A demon is talking within a minute, and it's funny and strange. That meets DAVID.md #1.
- **Dario.** He reacts to where you've been ("You've been to the Lock House. You smell like her soup." / "Count your fingers.") and to the shed split ("Half a door. I'm still working out whether that was clever or cruel."). The flirt option "Finish the line." → "There wasn't a rest of it. I had one line and I used it on a man who walks into sewers for fun." This is the homoerotic tone David wants, and it lands.
- **The drain-shed split.** Splitting the charges gets "as if you'd handed them half a bridge", and later a boom rolls through the rock: "Dario has found a use for his charge." That is a fixed event shaped by a choice, which is the SMT I bombs idea.
- **Catching Dario's lie with environmental evidence.** Bel's fact (the radio died in week one), plus the chalk radio log by the wall ("DAY 9 SHE SAYS COME"), then "You're lying" → "You caught Dario Venn in a lie." It feels good.
- **Furfur.** The scripted intro asks "top or bottom?" Calling him a liar, then drawing a triangle in the dust, makes him tell the truth and join. That was the best demon moment.
- **Kappa's bow** (it bows back and spills its dish) and **Gargoyle's garbled speech** ("Grrlbl... HALT! Glorrg... you... EVIL?"). Demons do feel distinct.
- **Press-turn sweeps once you know the weaknesses.** Kodama + Kappa died to Bash, Thunder Edge, Spark and Bash before they acted. Valkyrie's Glint vs the Light-weak Oni, Minotaur and Banshee felt great. The signatures have flavor: Bunbuku Teakettle, Riverbank Sumo, Sweet Puck's Luck (heals the whole party for ~45), Liar's Thunder, Gobble You Up, Weighing of the Heart.
- **Environmental storytelling** in signs: the Lock House "(She can hear you. She is choosing not to.)", the clerk's blotter "Residents of the Row to be told nothing.", the drain wheel "(Persons are in the cistern.)", Pell's tally marks "STILL HERE", "I TRIED. — P.H.", and the Banshee "singing for the man who sold the light."
- **The ending reflection.** It quotes your words back, then lists fates and your record: "You talked to everything that would listen… Up here, people call that stalling." / "Mercy is a position too. It costs whoever they walk into next." / "You ran from 13 fights." This is the Catherine/SMT I payoff, and it is the strongest page in the game.
- **Gonggong's greeting reads your hidden meters** (kills, mercy, fleeing). It is exactly the "NPCs react to stats you didn't think about" idea.
- **Art.** The Kenney tiles are consistent and the Ward is charming. Museum portraits like the Valkyrie (Arbo) and the Troll are lovely.

## Confusing or boring (where)
- **The Culverts and the Cistern: repetition.** The same Kodama + Kappa pair three times, Leprechaun five times, and the "special" kettle demon (Bake-danuki) as a random mob four times. DAVID.md names "meeting the same few demons over and over" as a dislike.
- **Weaknesses are forgotten** for any demon you don't own. I learned the Leprechaun was Phys-weak and the next Leprechaun showed "Phys ?" again. The same happened with the Basilisk, Oni and Golem. You re-probe every fight.
- **Backtracking is invisible and punished.** Pell's choice "Go home. Your mother needs to see you." gets no reply and no follow-up hint. The reunion scene and the Sump Row warning live at the Ward drain (map ward x19 y18–19), and Hester's confrontation lives back in the Culverts. Nothing points there, and the trip is about 150 steps of random fights.
- **The drain-shed choice fired before I had met Dario or Teo.** I had only talked to Ines, then walked toward the stairs, and the scene made me pick between Ines and a stranger. Gate it on having met all three leaders, or move the trigger.
- **There's nowhere to see what you're carrying or what you promised.** The charge, Teo's ledger, Hester's stone and the Stamped Wrench never appear in Items or the Journal. The stone and the wrench are never used, and the empty socket stone in the Undergate offers no "place the marker" option.
- **The Journal** lists lies as plain facts with no "who said it". Caught lies aren't marked, and entries go stale ("Pell Haldane is alive… trapped" after he went home). The intro text says "say so to their face", but without attribution you can't tell who to confront.
- **Choices with no reply.** "I'll think about it" (Ines), "Give me the ledger" (Teo), "I'll speak for the river" (Hester), "Go home" (Pell), "Leave it alone" (turbine intake), and pulling Teo's "DON'T" millrace lever all close the scene or show nothing. Many big decisions feel like nothing happened.
- **The theme is stated outright four times:** Ines ("There isn't room for both. Pick."), Bel's boat line (fine, that one is subtle), the pre-finale "There isn't room for all of it. There isn't room for all of them.", and the coda "There isn't room for everything." DAVID.md lists "obvious themes" as a dislike. Keep it for the coda only.
- **Pell exposes all three lies in one speech** (Ines' son, Teo's wrench, Dario's radio). That does the player's detective work for him. Let the chalk, the wrench and the survey do it.
- **Teo answers a question nobody asked:** "Why did the gate give? Old iron, friend… Nobody's fault." It reads as the game waving a flag. Also, his first two lines are in the wrong order ("Ines sent you?" comes before "There he is… Sit, sit.").

## Story and writing
- **Best lines:** "People in a flood remember who promised them a boat." (Bel) · "as if you'd handed them half a bridge" · "You've got a face people stare at. That's not a line. Well. It's a bit of a line." · "SHE WAS HERE FIRST." · "Residents of the Row to be told nothing. Culvert will hold indefinitely." · Furfur's "Darling, you're the handsomest man I've ever seen. *thunder rumbles* That one might even be true." · "Up here, people call that stalling." · "Mercy is a position too."
- **Weakest:** the repeated theme statements (see above), Pell's three-lie info dump, Teo's unprompted denial, and Gonggong's back-to-back "you killed a great many… we counted every one" / "you let Our children go… we do not forget a kindness" with no beat joining them (it reads as a contradiction rather than a judgement).
- **Did choices feel like they mattered?** In the moment, partly. The split got Dario's line and the boom. Taking the ledger and the stone counted as promises behind the scenes (I ended at the cap of 2, so river_home was allowed). Most choices produced no visible reaction, though, and the fates ignored much of what I did: no line for Pell (I rescued him), Bel, the caught lies, the wrench, the ledger or the stone. The rule in bible.json says "the narration shows why" when the player asks for something his record forbids. My finale text ("set my one charge under the wall… Sump Row gets a warning first") got a river-home narration that never explained why the warning or the wall plan didn't happen. That may be partly the mock director, so check it in live play.
- **Did the theme land?** Yes, emotionally, in the ending: "You gave the river back its room, and took it from the people who were living there." It is over-explained before that point.
- **Did the ending reflect what I did?** The meters did (kills, mercy, fled, lies, talks), and that part is excellent. My story choices and my words mostly did not.

## Combat and balance
- **Phys is almost always the answer early.** Leprechaun, Kodama, Basilisk and Amduscias are all Phys-weak, so Bash spam wins the Culverts. Late enemies are Light-weak (Oni, Minotaur, Troll, Banshee, Amduscias), and only Valkyrie has Glint. Spread the weaknesses wider.
- **Enemy press turns punish you hard.** Weakness hits give enemies extra actions. With the harness I took 46–96 per physical hit, and Puck (the healer) died to a single Oni or Golem Bash (105–135). That is SMT-faithful, but the protagonist's death ends the game even with demons standing, and there is no revive item in the shop.
- **Failed escapes are deadly.** "X strikes first!" gives the enemy a full turn. Escape failed about half the time in the Undergate.
- **HP-cost skills quietly drain the party.** Thunder Edge, Floodspout and Frost Edge cost HP. Kappa went to 9 HP from its own spells. The cost is shown, but it's easy to miss.
- **Strappy Harness.** It's a great joke and the flirt intent fits David perfectly. The shop says "Physical -1 (weak)" but not that weak means the enemy gets extra turns. You can only swap armor in town. Allow armor swaps at save points, or soften it to "-1 defense, not weak".
- **Gold glut.** I ended with 3,300 gold after the 800 tribute and 1,000+ in other demands. There is nothing to buy below ground.
- **Fusion stalls.** After the first fusion (Gargoyle + Brokkr → Valkyrie, which was great), 18 of 21 pairs show "???" (too strong), and the only visible result is a lower-level Oni. Show the result's name and level ("Lv 12 – need Lv 12") so fusion stays a goal.
- **Level-ups are silent.** Stat points are only discovered in the Party screen ("3 stat points to spend"), and skill-learn prompts are hidden there too. The protagonist still had only 2 skills (Bash, Mend) at Lv 11.
- **The difficulty curve is right for the Ward and Culverts,** comfortable in the Cistern, and spikes hard in the Undergate (Anubis Lv 12, Minotaur, Amduscias and Banshee Lv 11 at player Lv 9).

## Level design per map
- **The Ward:** charming and readable. The chapel door does nothing. The east bridge leading straight into the stairs enclosure is easy to miss, because walking down from the west bridge dead-ends at the rocks. The drain-shed trigger sits on the only path to the stairs, so it fires before you've met everyone.
- **The Culverts:** a good intro loop (key in the NE treasure room, locked door, sluice drains the south channel). A dead-end mystery door at (12,13). The entrance room is identical to the next two floors' entrance rooms (stairs, spring, save), so it feels copy-pasted.
- **Cistern Galleries:** the best puzzle. The gallery key is in the NE store room, the west door leads to the drain wheel, and the lake turns to shallows so you can reach the island and Pell. The clerk's office survey is perfect storytelling. The east walkway dead-ends at y16, which is fine.
- **Undergate Approach:** the pillars and the turbine hall choice set the mood. The key is in the far east room, and the spring and save are behind the key door. The west loop shortcut back to the start is good. Put a spring or save at the entrance, or move the key-door spring nearer the start.
- **The Undergate (source):** a short arena with a bridge to the boss. Fine. The boss portrait (a small ink sketch of a man) undersells "a man's face on a serpent's body, red hair drifting".

## UI notes
- The ASCII-free world screen is clean. The sidebar party HP and the Journal button are good.
- The talk screen needs a Leave button and visible patience or remaining turns. Show the demand box only while the demand is pending.
- The encounter screen's portrait is left-aligned while the name is centered (playtest-enc-kettle.png). It should show a "too strong to command" badge before you talk.
- The Items screen repeats the whole inventory under every party member (7×). Use one list plus a target picker.
- The Party screen needs demon HP, affinities, and your own weaknesses (needed to "protect your weaknesses").
- The header buttons wrap below long map names ("The Undergate Approach"), so the layout jumps.
- Map viewport: about 15×11 tiles is fine, but camera clamping leaves large black voids at the top-left of the Culverts, Cistern and Undergate entrances.

## Prioritized top-10 fix list
1. **Halve the random encounter rate** (add a grace period after fights and scenes) and **cap repeats per floor.** No demon should appear more than twice per floor, and Bake-danuki shouldn't be a random mob.
2. **Remember discovered affinities per species** across battles. Show them in the Compendium, on the Party screen (including your own and your demons' weaknesses), and in enemy rows.
3. **Signpost or relocate the return-to-Ward payoffs.** After Pell or the survey, add a Journal "Unfinished" list ("Sump Row hasn't been warned", "Pell went home"), or let you send a demon up to warn Sump Row from below. Make finale words like "warn Sump Row first" count, or have the narration say why it was too late. Mention Pell, Bel and caught lies in the fates.
4. **Fix the Undergate spike:** spring or save at the entrance, randoms capped near player level, and failed escape costs half a turn rather than a full ambush. Show the escape odds.
5. **Show the level gate before negotiating,** stop demands that can't end in a join, and refuse joins while a demand is unpaid.
6. **Make the boss need more than gold.** Use the carried story items (stone, wrench, ledger, charge) in the Gonggong talk and the finale, and give each item an acknowledgment. Show held key items and promises in Items or the Journal.
7. **Fix the UI bugs:** "null" headings, the Leave button in talk, status refresh after springs, stale messages, "You dodges", Valkyrie's "his", the "sump_row" raw id, "End of the prologue" ×3, the shop's duplicate text and overflow, opened-chest sprites, the repeating spring scene, the stale knock sign and the stale Pell Journal entry.
8. **Give every scene choice a one-line reaction,** and gate the drain-shed choice until you've met Ines, Teo and Dario.
9. **Say the theme once.** Cut it from Ines and from the pre-finale narration and keep the coda. Don't let Pell solve all three lies at once, and drop Teo's unprompted denial.
10. **Economy and progression:** a reason to spend gold below ground (a demon merchant, a revive item), armor swaps at save points, a clear warning on the Harness, named fusion results with required levels, and level-up and skill-learn notifications after battle.
